A practical handbook

Practice change without chasing perfection

Tend helps you define a small action, connect it to a stable cue, and record each day you show up. It is a focused practice tracker—not a task list or a promise that a habit forms on a fixed date.

01

Cue → action

Define one realistic practice

Choose a stable cue

Use an event that already happens: finishing breakfast, arriving home, or closing your laptop.

Name the action

Describe what you will do in concrete language so the practice is easy to recognize.

Make the minimum small

Choose a version that remains realistic on a difficult day. Doing more is always optional.

Example: After I put my breakfast plate in the sink, I will walk outside. On a difficult day, standing outside for one minute counts.

02

One quiet check

Record evidence, not a streak

Mark completed days

Each practice has its own start date and length. Select an available day after performing the action; select it again to correct a mistake.

Leave missed days neutral

An unmarked day stays quiet. It does not erase earlier practice, remove points, or block the next available day.

Use the matrix at a glance

Practices with the same duration are grouped together while preserving their independent calendar dates.

Extend when useful

You can lengthen an existing practice without losing its history. Existing practice windows cannot be shortened.

03

A private keepsake

Review and optionally share

Completion is a time boundary

A practice completes after its final calendar day, or immediately when its final day is marked. Completion does not claim the behavior is automatic.

Create a growth card

The optional image summarizes days tended. You can edit or hide the practice name and preview the exact image and caption before sharing.

Privacy: Production practice data is synchronized through your account and protected by PostgreSQL Row-Level Security. Only the growth-card image and caption are shared when you choose a share or download action.

04

Research basis

What informed the design

Tend translates habit research into modest product choices. Habit formation varies greatly between people and behaviors, so a selected practice length is a motivating frame rather than a scientific deadline.

  • Gardner, Lally & Wardle — Making health habitual
  • Lally et al. — Modelling habit formation in the real world
  • Wood & Rünger — Psychology of Habit
  • Phillips & Hine — Self-compassion and health behaviour meta-analysis

Health boundary: Tend is not treatment. Behaviors involving addiction, eating disorders, self-harm, compulsions, or serious medical risk deserve support from an appropriately qualified professional.
